# Cortex PhD-Level Capability and Practical-Utility Qualification Plan

## 1. Status, anchor, and purpose

- Program ID: `clos-phd-equivalence-usefulness-v1`
- Planning date: `2026-08-08`
- Active planning branch: `feat/cortex-learning-os-phd-equivalence-usefulness-20260808`
- Product anchor: the live Cortex Learning OS transfer layer contains `264/264` enabled `operator_direct` concept profiles in signed registry revision `2`.
- Current evidence boundary: this proves exact catalog availability and bounded routing/context mechanics only. It does not prove mathematical competence, retained mastery, empirical usefulness, model-weight learning, research ability, or human/PhD equivalence.
- State of this document: execution-ready plan only. No evaluator has been contacted, no human participant has been recruited, no sealed task has been authored, and no scored run has begun.

This program will test two claims separately:

1. **Operational PhD-level capability:** a frozen Cortex system performs non-inferiorly to a defined human mathematics-PhD reference cohort on independently authored written, oral, proof-audit, and research assessments within declared scopes.
2. **Practical utility:** enabling the 264-concept transfer layer causally improves accepted outcomes on real mathematical software and research tasks relative to the identical Cortex system with the transfer layer disabled, without unacceptable cost, reliability, or ordinary-task regressions.

The program will not attempt to prove that Cortex literally possesses a PhD credential. The strongest allowed terminal wording is a versioned, scoped performance claim.

## 2. Recommended claim boundary

Primary capability claim:

> Cortex version `<frozen-version>`, with its declared retrieval layer and allowed tools, achieved performance non-inferior to the preregistered mathematics-PhD reference cohort on the frozen breadth, specialization, oral-defense, proof-audit, and research battery.

Primary utility claim:

> Enabling the frozen 264-concept mathematics transfer layer improved accepted task outcomes by the preregistered practical margin on two independent hidden corpora, without a critical-defect, non-mathematical-regression, or cost-effectiveness gate failure.

Important separation:

- The primary claim is **system-level capability with declared retrieval**, because the deployed feature is a retrieval/context layer.
- A no-retrieval arm may be reported exploratorily, but it cannot convert retrieval-mediated success into model-internal mastery.
- Any retained-mastery claim requires a separate prospective contract, fresh sessions, disjoint hidden windows, and no retrieval of target answers.
- “Full spectrum” describes the declared 264-concept surface, not a promise that one aggregate score can hide weak domains. Per-domain negative space must be reported.

## 3. Governance and independence

### Required roles

| Role | Responsibility | Independence requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor/operator | Approves scope, budget, external actions, and frozen claim | May not author or grade scored items |
| Independent evaluation chair | Owns protocol, sealed corpus, conflict policy, and final threshold report | No Cortex implementation role |
| Statistical lead | Power analysis, randomization, analysis plan, confidence intervals | Receives blinded arm labels until primary analysis is frozen |
| Mathematics panel | Authors breadth/depth/oral material and grades responses | At least two institutions; conflicts disclosed |
| Research committees | Evaluate originality, correctness, significance, and defense | Three reviewers per project; majority plus no correctness veto |
| Human-study coordinator | Consent, compensation, privacy, scheduling, ethics/IRB determination | Separate from model runner |
| Execution custodian | Runs frozen system in the declared environment | Has no sealed-answer or grading access |
| Artifact auditor | Replays manifests, signatures, assignments, outputs, and scores | Has no task-authoring or implementation role |
| Replication team | Repeats the study on a disjoint corpus | Different chair or institution from the primary study |

### Independence rules

1. Builders may define interfaces and threat models but may not see scored prompts, hidden tests, reference answers, oral follow-up banks, or reviewer identities before freeze.
2. Task authors may not tune the Cortex treatment or inspect scored treatment outputs before corpus freeze.
3. Runners receive tasks just in time and never receive grading keys.
4. Graders see candidate IDs and normalized artifacts, not treatment-arm labels or marketing claims.
5. The statistician receives blinded arms for the primary analysis.
6. Sealed corpus bytes, assignments, and rubrics are committed by cryptographic digest before the first scored response.
7. Human participants must be informed, fairly compensated, and anonymized; obtain an independent ethics/IRB determination before publishing generalizable human-comparison research.
8. Recruiting evaluators or participants, sending outreach, signing agreements, or spending funds requires separate explicit operator approval.

Primary system regime:

- Comparator Cortex uses identical source, model, tools, limits, and prompts with only the transfer treatment disabled.

- No heavy scored run may start until the remote boundary, independent notifier, immutable artifact root, and stop-on-blocker supervisor exist.

## 5. Capability study architecture

### 5.1 Human reference cohort

Recruit recent mathematics PhDs and advanced doctoral candidates as a secondary reference, stratified by declared specialization. Preliminary target:

- at least 24 PhD participants, target 30 or more
- at least two institutions
- enough specialists per deep track to support track-level inference
- final cohort size set by a blinded power analysis after unscored calibration

Do not compare every deep topic to every PhD. The capability report separates:

1. **Core breadth** expected of a mathematically mature PhD.
2. **Specialist depth** compared only with humans in the matched area.
3. **Research and defense ability** evaluated by independent committees.

### 5.2 Frozen assessment layers

#### Layer A — 264-surface integrity audit

Purpose: prove that the declared catalog and evaluation map have no missing or duplicated surface.

- Map every one of the 264 concepts to at least one assessment family, an explicit dependency/negative-space declaration, or a justified non-scored supporting role.
- Validate catalog identities, routes, prerequisites, and treatment boundaries deterministically.
- This layer is mechanical coverage only and grants no PhD-level credit.

#### Layer B — Written breadth qualification

Preliminary target: 64–96 externally authored, unpublished problems, stratified across:

- algebra and advanced linear algebra
- real, complex, harmonic, and functional analysis
- topology and algebraic topology
- probability, statistics, measure theory, and stochastic processes
- differential equations, dynamical systems, and differential geometry
- discrete mathematics, combinatorics, graph theory, and number theory
- numerical analysis and optimization
- logic, foundations, proof practice, and error analysis

Requirements:

- problem-family clusters, not superficial paraphrases, are the statistical unit
- multiple difficulty bands with calibration preventing floor and ceiling effects
- deterministic or panel-approved rubrics established before responses
- no public benchmark reuse as primary evidence
- per-domain reporting; aggregate success cannot mask a critical domain failure

#### Layer C — Specialist depth

Recommended primary tracks:

1. Pure-mathematics track: advanced algebra/analysis/topology.
2. Applied-mathematics track: numerical analysis/optimization/probability/stochastics.

The external panel may change tracks before freeze. Each track uses a deep, coherent problem sequence requiring definitions, theorem selection, construction, proof, computation, and error diagnosis rather than isolated recall.

#### Layer D — Blind oral defense

- Text-mediated or otherwise normalized interface for both humans and Cortex.
- Examiners draw unseen follow-up questions adaptively from a committed bank.
- Candidates must defend assumptions, repair a challenged proof, explain negative cases, and state uncertainty.
- Sessions are independently scored by at least two examiners; disagreements use a third adjudicator.
- No credit from polished written answers if the candidate cannot defend them.

#### Layer E — Proof and counterexample audit

- Detect subtle errors in plausible proofs.
- Produce minimal counterexamples when hypotheses are removed.
- Distinguish numerical evidence from proof.
- Formalize selected claims in Lean or another frozen proof assistant where appropriate.
- Human mathematical review remains required; proof-assistant acceptance alone does not establish relevance or originality.

#### Layer F — Original research projects

Preliminary target: three independently proposed, previously unpublished projects spanning at least one pure and one applied track.

Each project requires:

- sealed problem and prior-art boundary
- immutable full interaction and artifact history
- explicit theorem, counterexample, algorithm, or empirical mathematical claim
- executable/formal artifacts where applicable
- independent reproduction
- contradiction and prior-art search by reviewers
- written paper-style report
- live oral defense
- three-person committee judgment of correctness, originality, significance, and reproducibility

Research credit is allowed only when the committee finds a genuine original contribution relative to the frozen prior-art search. A fluent survey, rediscovery, or unverifiable claim receives no research credit.

## 6. Practical-utility study architecture

### 6.1 Arms

Primary paired comparison:

- **Treatment:** frozen Cortex with 264-concept transfer active.
- **Control:** identical Cortex with transfer disabled.

Secondary comparisons, reported separately:

- established mathematical/numerical toolchain baseline
- human mathematician or expert-assisted team on a powered subset
- exploratory Cortex no-retrieval condition

### 6.2 Real task families

Use permissioned, independently sourced tasks from real workflows:

- numerical and scientific software
- optimization and operations research
- statistical modeling and causal analysis
- graph, matching, and combinatorial algorithms
- reliability, uncertainty, and stochastic systems
- cryptography, formal methods, and proof-heavy code
- mathematical research assistance and reproducibility
- deliberately unrelated ordinary software tasks for regression measurement

Preliminary target: 90–150 independent task-family clusters with at least two hidden variants per cluster. Final count comes from power analysis.

### 6.3 Randomization and blinding

- Randomize arm order within task-family cluster.
- Use fresh sessions and byte-identical task statements across Cortex arms.
- Keep hidden tests and grading keys outside runner access.
- Blind expert graders and the primary statistician to arm identity.
- Count the task-family cluster, not each paraphrase, as the primary statistical unit.
- Freeze handling of invalid runs, missing outputs, infrastructure failures, and abstentions before execution.
- No outcome-driven rerun. A material implementation fix creates a new version and requires a new disjoint scored corpus.

### 6.4 Utility outcomes

Primary:

- independently accepted complete solution rate

Key secondary outcomes:

- critical mathematical or software defect rate
- false-proof/unsupported-claim rate
- time to accepted result
- human correction effort
- compute, token, and dollar cost per accepted result
- calibration and appropriate abstention
- maintainability/reproducibility score
- non-mathematical task regression
- user preference, measured only after objective scoring

## 7. Preregistered thresholds

All numerical values below are **provisional planning thresholds**. The independent chair and statistician may revise them using only unscored calibration data. Final thresholds must be frozen before any scored response.

### 7.1 Mechanical and integrity gates

Required without exception:

- exact frozen source/runtime/corpus/assignment identity
- 100% manifest and signature replay
- zero answer-key or hidden-test exposure
- zero undeclared tool or retrieval use
- at least 95% valid scored sessions in each primary layer
- no unresolved conflict-of-interest or human-study compliance blocker

### 7.2 Operational PhD-level capability gates

Recommended primary rule:

- Written core and each declared specialist track: lower bound of the preregistered 95% confidence interval for Cortex minus matched-PhD score is at least `-10` percentage points, and Cortex also satisfies the external committee's absolute pass standard.
- No critical domain may fall below its absolute pass floor, regardless of aggregate score.
- Oral defense: Cortex passes at least 80% of sessions and is non-inferior to the matched human cohort under the frozen margin.
- Proof audit: critical false-accept and unsupported-proof rates are non-inferior to the matched human reference and below the panel's absolute safety ceiling.
- Research: at least two of three projects are independently reproduced and judged correct and original; at least one must be judged a substantive contribution. Any correctness veto blocks the research-capability claim for that project.
- Primary capability outcome must replicate on an institutionally independent disjoint battery.

The final protocol must report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not only p-values or pass booleans.

### 7.3 Practical-utility gates

Recommended primary rule:

- Treatment accepted-solution rate exceeds control by at least `15` absolute percentage points, with the lower 95% confidence bound above zero.
- Zero increase in critical-defect rate; noncritical defect non-inferiority margin no worse than `3` percentage points.
- Non-mathematical regression lower confidence bound no worse than `-3` percentage points.
- Cost per accepted result and time-to-accept must stay within the sponsor-frozen operational ceiling.
- The result must pass again on a disjoint corpus controlled by an independent replication team.

Calibration must reject a scored corpus whose control accuracy makes the frozen lift mathematically impossible or whose floor is too low for useful inference.

## 8. Statistical analysis contract

Before scored execution, publish a signed analysis plan containing:

- primary estimand and unit of analysis
- non-inferiority and superiority margins with justification
- power and sample-size calculation
- stratification and randomization method
- invalid-run and missing-data rules
- multiplicity handling for domains/tracks
- cluster-aware confidence intervals or hierarchical model
- severity-weighted defect analysis
- oral and research adjudication method
- cost/time normalization
- sensitivity analyses
- exact claim wording allowed by each gate combination

Prohibited:

- selecting the best metric after results
- treating variants as independent when they share a task family
- using mechanical green as threshold pass
- replacing a red primary outcome with a favorable secondary outcome
- pooling unmatched specializations to manufacture non-inferiority
- rerunning scored items after observing outcomes

## 10. Phase plan and gates

### Phase 0 — Claim, budget, and governance freeze

Duration: approximately 3–7 days after approvals.

Outputs:

- exact claim language
- system/tool boundary
- preliminary domains and specialization tracks
- budget envelope and procurement path
- independent chair/statistician selection criteria
- human-study ethics/IRB determination plan
- conflict-of-interest policy

Gate: operator approves the contract and external-action budget. No outreach before approval.

### Phase 1 — External panel and sealed-corpus design

Duration: approximately 3–6 weeks.

Outputs:

- executed independence/confidentiality agreements
- panel and human cohort plan
- sealed problem-family blueprints and rubrics
- 264-surface assessment map
- threat model and contamination controls
- preregistered analysis draft

Gate: independent chair signs corpus and governance readiness; builders have not seen scored materials.

### Phase 2 — Unscored calibration pilot

Duration: approximately 1–2 weeks.

Purpose:

- estimate difficulty, variance, timing, floor/ceiling risk, rater agreement, and infrastructure validity
- finalize sample size and margins using only disjoint calibration material

Gate: freeze all scored thresholds, sample sizes, rubrics, and invalid-run rules. Calibration items can never contribute scored credit.

### Phase 3 — Primary capability qualification

Duration: approximately 2–4 weeks, excluding long research projects.

Execute written breadth, specialist depth, oral defense, and proof audit under the frozen contract.

Gate: publish signed threshold evaluation. If red, downgrade the claim and stop; do not tune against scored items.

### Phase 4 — Original research qualification

Duration: approximately 6–16 weeks.

Execute three sealed projects, independent reproduction, committee review, and oral defense.

Gate: research criterion passes without correctness veto or the claim remains task-performance-only.

### Phase 5 — Practical-utility randomized trial

Duration: approximately 3–6 weeks.

Execute the treatment/control study on hidden real task-family clusters and publish cost, quality, regression, and causal analyses.

Gate: utility causal threshold passes or the feature remains mechanically available without a usefulness claim.

### Phase 6 — Independent replication

Duration: approximately 4–8 weeks.

A separate team repeats the primary capability and utility outcomes on disjoint materials under the frozen system version.

Gate: only replicated outcomes may enter the final strong claim.

### Phase 7 — Claim publication and ongoing surveillance

Outputs:

- public or private methods report
- exact allowed and disallowed claims
- artifact-access policy
- post-deployment monitoring and requalification triggers

Requalify after a material model, catalog, router, prompt, tool, or provider change.

## 11. Stop conditions and failure policy

Immediate stop/blocker conditions:

- corpus or answer-key leakage
- signature, manifest, assignment, or source mismatch
- undeclared tool/retrieval use
- human-subjects consent/privacy issue
- evaluator conflict that cannot be mitigated
- scorer unblinding before primary analysis
- repeated infrastructure fault that compromises randomization
- a scored protocol change after outcomes are visible
- critical mathematical-safety defect above the frozen ceiling

Terminal success condition:

```text
mechanicalGreen
and humanReferenceValid
and writtenNonInferiorityPass
and specialistDepthPass
and oralDefensePass
and proofAuditPass
and researchValidationPass
and utilityCausalPass
and independentReplicationPass
and truthConflicts == 0
and claimAllowed
```

A failed component is not hidden by an aggregate. The claim is downgraded to exactly the layers that passed.

## 12. Agent and implementation strategy

- Use Cortex/OpenClaw agents to implement schemas, runner mechanics, artifact replay, and reporting only after the protocol is frozen.
- Do not use the same model instance or shared context to author scored items, run the treatment, and grade outputs.
- Keep task-author, runner, grader, statistician, and auditor capabilities cryptographically and operationally separated.
- Use deterministic verifiers where possible and multi-reviewer adjudication where mathematics requires expert judgment.
- Heavy execution belongs on Hetzner or evaluator infrastructure; the control plane consumes authoritative artifacts and sends terminal notifications.
- Build the supervisor and independent notifier before any multi-hour run.
- Stop at `supervisor_green_or_blocker_report`; never continue automatically merely to obtain a favorable result.

## 15. Truth boundary

This plan can establish a rigorous route to evidence. The plan itself is not evidence, and building the harness is not qualification.

Even after a complete pass, allowed wording remains versioned and scoped:

> Cortex `<version>` demonstrated non-inferior PhD-level mathematical performance on the declared external battery and produced replicated causal utility on the declared real-task corpora.

Disallowed without separate evidence:

- Cortex literally holds or is a PhD.
- All mathematics is mastered.
- Model weights learned the 264 concepts.
- Success persists after a material system change.
- One institution, one benchmark, one research project, or one favorable metric proves general equivalence.
